    What is a Gaussian splat?

    Every model in this archive is a Gaussian splat, a way of recording real objects and spaces in 3D from ordinary photographs or video.

    Instead of building a model from flat surfaces and textures, as traditional 3D software does, a Gaussian splat describes a scene as a cloud of tiny, soft, semi-transparent blobs of colour. Each blob, or “splat”, has its own position, size, shape, orientation and transparency. On their own they look like fuzzy specks. Viewed together, tens of thousands of them blend into a continuous, photographic image that holds together as you move around it.

    The splats are calculated by software that compares many images of the same subject taken from different angles, and adjusts every splat until the 3D scene matches what the camera saw. Because the method works directly from photographs, it captures qualities that are hard to model by hand, such as the grain of card and timber, the fuzz of model trees, soft shadows and the way light falls across a surface.

    The result is a record of the object as it looked on the day it was captured, not a CAD drawing. It shows what a model looks like rather than precise measurements, and very shiny or transparent materials such as glass and acrylic can appear blurred. The scenes here are stored in SOG, a compressed format that keeps most models to around a megabyte.
